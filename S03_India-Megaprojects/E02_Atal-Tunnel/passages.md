### P01

For about six months every winter, snow closed the road over Rohtang Pass, in the Himalayas. The valleys of Lahaul and Spiti were cut off from the rest of the country.

So engineers asked a different question. Not how to clear the snow, but how to go underneath it.

### P02

The Atal Tunnel is nine point oh two kilometres long. The Press Information Bureau says it was recognised in 2022 as the world's longest highway tunnel above ten thousand feet. It cut the Manali to Sarchu distance by forty-six kilometres, and the travel time by four to five hours.

### P03

Two more numbers. The decision to build it was taken in June 2000. The tunnel was dedicated to the nation on the third of October 2020. Twenty years, for nine kilometres. Rock does not hurry.

### P04

Idea one: reading the rock. A mountain is not one material. Think of cutting a cake when you cannot see inside: here a soft layer, there a nut. You cannot plan every cut in advance.

The builders chose drill and blast, so they could change the support as the digging went on. Before each blast, the rock is a guess. After it, the rock tells you what support it needs.

### P05

Idea two: air. Think of a room with no windows and a car engine running. The air turns bad in minutes. A long tunnel at this height needs fans to move the air in and out.

This one uses a semi-transverse system, where large fans circulate air along the tunnel. The designers had to allow for different air pressure at each end, and for the seasons.

### P06

Idea three: a way out, and a way to know. A tunnel this long cannot rely on one safeguard. The Press Information Bureau's factsheet lists exit signs every twenty-five metres, a telephone every one hundred and fifty, fire hydrants every sixty, cameras that detect incidents every two hundred and fifty, and air-quality monitors every kilometre.

Think of the exit signs in a theatre. They are not for the show. They are for the worst night.

### P07

Now, what India built. The Border Roads Organisation built it. The hardest stretch was a fault zone, five hundred and eighty-seven metres of weak rock, called Seri Nalah. The breakthrough from both ends came on the fifteenth of October 2017.

And what a tunnel cannot do. It removes one barrier. It is not the whole route.

### P08

Tunnels are measured in many ways: length, height above sea level, width, number of tubes. The Atal Tunnel's record is a specific one: a highway tunnel, above ten thousand feet. Change the measure, and the list changes. These are measures, not a league table.

### P09

A fictional winter evening: snow is falling at one end. A driver enters the tunnel, and for nine kilometres the road ignores the weather. A tunnel does not remove winter. It moves the road out of it.

### P10

One idea about people: the planning fallacy. We think a hard job will take less time than it does. The decision to tunnel under this pass was taken in 2000. The two ends met in 2017. Rock is a surprise generator, and a plan that ignores surprises runs late.

### P11

Three things to remember. A mountain is read as it is cut, so support is decided as the digging goes. A long tunnel needs air and a way out, not only a road. And a barrier removed is one barrier, not the whole route.

India built the tunnel. Now the question is the road beyond it: which pass is next?

### P01

Imagine a road where trucks, buses and scooters all share one lane. Everyone moves at the speed of the slowest. Now imagine a lane built only for trucks.

That is the idea behind India's dedicated freight corridors.

### P02

The corridors are railway lines built only for goods trains. The Ministry of Railways says the Dedicated Freight Corridor project enables double-stack container trains, trains with a higher axle load, and faster access from the western ports to the northern hinterland.

The Eastern corridor runs one thousand three hundred and thirty-seven kilometres, from Ludhiana to Sonnagar. The Western runs one thousand five hundred and six, from Dadri to the Jawaharlal Nehru Port.

### P03

There is a reason to care that is not about freight. The Ministry says the corridors have created additional paths on the old network, because goods trains moved to the new lines. Passenger and goods services on the old tracks could then grow.

### P04

Idea one: separate lanes. Think of the truck-only lane. The trucks stop slowing the buses, and the buses stop slowing the trucks.

Before a dedicated line, a goods train waits for a passenger train to pass, and the reverse. After it, each kind of train has its own track.

### P05

Idea two: double-stack. Think of stacking two books instead of one. The same train carries twice the boxes, if the wires and tunnels leave room.

In 2019, the Ministry said that wagons on such a long-haul train can carry four times the container units of the traffic then running on Indian Railways.

### P06

Idea three: weight. Think of standing on one foot, then on two. The same body presses harder on the floor through one foot. A wagon with a heavier load on each axle presses harder on the track, so the track must be stronger.

The corridor wagons are designed for an axle load of twenty-five tonnes. The Ministry said newer wagons could carry up to eighty-one tonnes at a hundred kilometres an hour, against sixty-one to seventy-one tonnes at about sixty.

### P07

Now, what India built, and what is not yet built. The Ministry says both corridors have been commissioned, with about four hundred and forty-three trains a day on average.

But a new corridor, from Dankuni in the east to Surat in the west, was only announced in the Union Budget of 2026. The Ministry says work on its detailed project report has begun. An announcement is not a railway.

### P08

A freight corridor is not a passenger line, and it is not a highway. Each carries a different thing, at a different speed. These are tools, not ranks.

### P09

A fictional night: a container leaves a port, rides the corridor, and reaches an inland terminal without waiting for a passenger express to pass. The corridor did not make the train fly. It stopped the train from waiting.

### P10

One idea about people: averages hide bottlenecks. A trip with a good average speed can still lose its time in one slow section. A trade publication reported that the average freight train speed was expected to rise from about twenty-six to about seventy kilometres an hour. That is an expectation, not a measurement.

### P11

Three things to remember. A separate lane stops everyone slowing everyone. A double stack and a heavier axle carry more per train, if track, wires and tunnels allow. And an announcement is not a railway.

India built the corridors. Now the question is the connections: how will the freight reach the corridor?
